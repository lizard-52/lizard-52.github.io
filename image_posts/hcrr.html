Halton County Radial Railway Fall 2026

September 28, 2026:

As a fan of streetcars and public transit in general I have wanted to go look at the largest electric railway museum in Canada a ever since I learned about it.

I didn't get good pictures of everything, they have a lot more in a big building as well as several vehicles wrapped in plastic (or just rotting) out back. For more info you can check out the Halton County Railway Museum website.

Original TTC subway rolling stock from the 1950s. Allegedly still operational, but it looks like it would be a huge pain to get it out of the building it's in.

Inside of same car. Interesting to see the little barrier things, you don't see those in modern trains.

Cab of same car.

'Peter Witt' streetcar. These were used from ~1920-1970.

Inside of same car. It was pretty busy when I visited, but there's lots of seating room in these.

Inside of a PCC car. Used from ~1940-1980

ICTS S Series car from Line 3, similar to what some of the Vancouver Sky Train uses. I miss you Line 3! Killed to soon :(

WHO SPENDS PILES OF MONEY ON A RAPID TRANSIT LINE AS A 'TEMPARARY' PIECE OF INFRASTRUCTRE! LIKE WHAT ARE WE EVEN DOING? THE TTC CLAIMED THAT THE LINE WAS OLD AND UNSAFE (AND ALSO COSTLY TO MAINTAIN), BUT VANCOUVER HAS BEEN OPERATING A VERY SIMIAR SYSTEM IN BOTH AGE AND TECHNOLOGY WITH AN EXCELLENT TRACK RECORD. LIKE YEAH THERE WAS A DERAILMENT A FEW MONTHS BEFORE THE SCHEDULED CLOSURE DATA, BUT THE INVESTIGATION LATER SHOWED THAT IT WAS DUE TO POOR MAINTENCE. IT'S LIKE WEAPONIZED INCOPETENCE BUT FOR TRANSIT INFRASTRUCTURE!!

For a bit of background, Line 3 was originally pitched as a 'temporary' extension to the end of Line 2 until demand increased and Line 2 was extended. It was planed to be closed in November 2023, but on July 24 2023 one car derailed causing 5 injuries and so was closed early. Has Line 2 been extended to cover the route that Line 3 did? Nope! The current plan is for that to be done in 2033. Some of the old Line 3 tracks are being replaced with a bus way, so that's nice I guess.

Lots of these are unlit. Also you generally can't go and sit in the seats.

A CLRV (Canadian Light Rail Vehicle). These were retired in 2019 and the museum got several, along with a bunch of parts.

An old station building, though no longer at it's original location. This looks like a CLRV, but it's actually a ALRV (Articulated Light Rail Vehicle). You can tell because of the bendy bit in the middle.
