The Hydrogen Sonata by Iain M. Banks

May 24, 2026

This book really feels like it's going nowhere for a big chunk of the middle. I felt that the plot was close to wrapping up at the ~60% mark, but yet things keep happening. I will not comment on the ended since there's not really anything I can say without spoilers, but I thought it was ok. I really enjoyed the more in-depth character development of some of the ships in this book. Overall a fun and engaging book that presents some interesting ideas along with some unexpected twists. I had fun reading it.

Hollow Knight

May 14, 2026:

As someone who learned about HK lore before playing the game I would recommend watching the 77 minute lore breakdowns after playing the game. Already knowing the story really reduces the amount of fun in discovering new areas and finding tablets. That being said I really enjoyed my playthrough regardless. I might even come back to get the 'good' ending.

I found the lack of difficulty options a problem. I'm not great at platformers and if it wasn't for the easy mode mod I would have given up at the Watcher Knights. Other than that one fight I felt like the boss fights were in roughly the correct difficulty range for me (though it did take me ~44.3 hours to get the bad ending), and were generally fun and rewarding to fight. Obviously the experienced difficulty is going to vary between players and what may be a reasonable difficulty for me might be far to hard for someone with little/no gaming experience. I don't think a single difficulty mode is a good design decision. The traditional advice of "get good" is... unhelpful. I do not find grinding the same boss over and over for hours at a time very fun, and fun is kinda the whole point of playing a game.

SCP-8980 by Yossipossi

April 22, 2026: The most terrifying an memorable SCP I have read in a long time. SCP-8980 uses a very different sort of horror to the more typical "evil entity that is going to kill everyone horribly" article. Definitely read the content warning before going on to the main body of the article.

My only complaint is that the format is a bit odd (hovering the highlighted text makes the corresponding footnote appear), but it's actually pretty normal for a more modern SCP entry all things considered.

If you have not read an SCP before I would recommend giving SCP-173, SCP-1000, and 'We Need To Talk About Fifty-Five' a quick read first to get an idea of the site.

Site 17 / 10

Iluminae by Amie Kaufman and Jay Kristoff

April 9, 2026: I first read this book in ~2016 after checking it out from the school library on recommendation of a friend. I really enjoyed it at the time, enough so to buy the next two books in the trilogy when they came out. As such I have never owned a copy of Iluminae. That is, until a week ago when I saw as used copy for sale at my local grocery store for $1. Curious to see if a "young adult[1] space opera epistolary novel" from a decade ago was as good as I remembered I immediately place Iluminae at the top of my queue. Short answer: almost. I remembered the broad plot elements, but was pleasantly surprised at all the things I had missed/forgotten from the first reading that make the character motivations more explainable. Also most of the science is ok, but which is neat for a book set ~500 years in the future. The two main characters are also ok, but the side characters are really where the writing shines ("Am I not merciful?"). Overall it's a great book with some very interesting typography and formatting decisions. Certainly more memorable than anything else I read in 2016.

Alexander-78V / 10

Project Hail Mary

April 6, 2026: As someone who is a big fan of the book I was a bit apprehensive about going to see the movie; I've seen enough movies that completely miss the point of the novel they are based one. Project Hail Mary does not have this fault. Other than a few visual or superficial changes (the design of the Hail Mary is worse than the book imo) nearly everything from the book is here, though some things are reduced to a quick mention instead of a multi chapter side plot. I was a bit concerned that some of the concepts weren't being explained enough, but the friends I was with who hadn't seen any trailers or read the book also very much enjoyed it so the writers must have struck a good balance in terms of exposition. Speaking of trailers, they certainly share information that I think makes the story less impactful.

Line 4 / 10

Matter by Iain M. Banks

March 11, 2026: I really like the general idea of this book. A lot of Culture books involve The Culture manipulating a less technologically advanced civilization that is unaware of the fact that it is being influenced, but that is not the case in this story. The Sarl, the subject of this novel, have technology comparably to humanity in the late 19th century. They have regular communication with multiple other alien civilizations which creates some very interesting dynamics. Setting the story on the Arithmetic, Mottled, Disputed, Multiply Inhabited, Multi-million-year Safe, Godded Shellworld of Sursamen also adds a bunch of neat concepts and ideas.
