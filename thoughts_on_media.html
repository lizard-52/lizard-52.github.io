My thoughts on media I consume; presented in reverse chronological order. Spoiler free.

Iluminae by Amie Kaufman and Jay Kristoff

April 9, 2026: I first read this book in ~2016 after checking it out from the school library on recommendation of a friend. I really enjoyed it at the time, enough so to buy the next two books in the trilogy when they came out. As such I have never owned a copy of Iluminae. That is, until a week ago when I saw as used copy for sale at my local grocery store for $1. Curious to see if a "young adult[1] space opera epistolary novel" from a decade ago was as good as I remembered I immediately place Iluminae at the top of my queue. Short answer: yes. I remembered the broad plot elements, but was pleasantly surprised at all the things I had missed/forgotten from the first reading that make the character motivations more explainable and clear. Overall it's a great book with some very interesting typography and formatting decisions.

Project Hail Mary

April 6, 2026: As someone who is a big fan of the book I was a bit apprehensive about going to see the movie; I've seen enough movies that completely miss the point of the novel they are based one. Project Hail Mary does not have this fault. Other than a few visual or superficial changes (the design of the Hail Mary is worse than the book imo) nearly everything from the book is here, though some things are reduced to a quick mention instead of a multi chapter side plot. I was a bit concerned that some of the concepts weren't being explained enough, but the friends I was with who hadn't seen any trailers or read the book also very much enjoyed it so the writers must have struck a good balance in terms of exposition. Speaking of trailers, they certainly share information that I think makes the story less impactful.

Astrophage / 10

Shada by Douglas Adams and Gareth Roberts

April 2, 2026: Fun and self contained. I'm not sure if it would be a better or worse experience if I knew more about the Doctor Who franchise, but much like Andor doesn't require Starwars Background knowledge Shada doesn't require background Doctor Who knowledge to be enjoyable. I much liked the reveal at the end. It is telegraphed enough that you can solve the mystery yourself before all of the characters have which is always fun.

Type 12 / 10

Hoppers

Mar 28, 2026: Decently entertaining. I was not bored. However, I don't think I will remember much about this movie in 6-12 months. Also I didn't really like the ending, but I suppose it's fine for a kids movie.

7 / 10

Calculating God by Robert J. Sawyer

Mar 20, 2026: Alright, but some of the science/evolutionary biology is very out of date. I also did not like the ending very much. Neat to have a book set in Toronto though.

Line 4 / 10

Matter by Iain M. Banks

Mar 11, 2026: I really like the general idea of this book. A lot of Culture books involve The Culture manipulating a less technologically advanced civilization that is unaware of the fact that it is being influenced, but that is not the case in this story. The Sarl, the subject of this novel, have technology comparably to humanity in the late 19th century. They have regular communication with multiple other alien civilizations which creates some very interesting dynamics. Setting the story on the Arithmetic, Mottled, Disputed, Multiply Inhabited, Multi-million-year Safe, Godded Shellworld of Sursamen also adds a bunch of neat concepts and ideas.
