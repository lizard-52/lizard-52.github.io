Line 5: Line 6 but better

July 18, 2026:

Line 5 and Line 6 opened at around the same time and are the last remains of the transit city plan from 2007 before it was brutally gutted by Rob Ford. Yes this does mean it took ~19 years for the two lines to go from plans to service. Of course both lines were significantly over budget too, but I don't really want to get into the weeds as to why that happened.

Line 5 is also know as the Eglinton Crosstown, and as the name suggests it runs either along or under Eglinton Ave from Kenedy to Mount Dennis. I rode the length of the line from east to west in two trips, after the first was halted by the worst words a TTC rider can hear: "shuttle buses are running" (there was an unspecified mechanical issue turning trains around at Cedarvale that also caused our train to be stuck in for like ~15 minutes).

Map of Line 5 (Orange). The orange/black section is underground.

I arrived at Kennedy Station by Go Train. The connection between the Go Train and Lines 2 and 5 isn't amazing, with a pretty long walk through a long, leaky and bleak corridor. It is well lit and clean, but the design language is very flat and I'm not a fan. Connection to Line 2 is pretty fast, I took ~70 seconds to walk from platform to platform.

Kennedy Station

The trip from Kennedy to Larid was pretty much as expected. The eastern part of Line 5 is very similar to Line 6 and shares many of the same problems. We had to wait at 4 (four) different red lights, four more than a train should ever have to stop at. Slow accelerating trains, slow speed limits, short station spacing, and suboptimal integration into the urban fabric are also present.

I got off at Larid to explore an underground suburban station. All the underground stations look very similar, and I find the design quite boring. Larid is very far underground, with some really long escalators required to get to the surface. The area right around the station is pretty nice, with shops and mid rise apartments packed pretty densely packed along Eglington. Frequency is quite good (~5-7min) so the wait was not long for the next train. However the departure board is full of lies, and the next train arrived when the screen showed '3min' until the next train.

Larid station platform
Outside Larid

The underground stretches of the line are pretty boring. I got off at Eglinton station to check the connection with Line 1. It was packed! It is good to see people using Line 5, I didn't see nearly as many people anywhere along Line 6. I found the layout of the station a bit confusing, but the signage is very helpful. A walk between platforms took me ~50 seconds.

The only splash of colour at Eglinton.
Most of it is the same boring asthetic.

Cedarvale station was quite chaotic with all the trains being turned around. Staff were present to tell people where to go to find the shuttle busses. It took me ~130 seconds to walk between the Line 5 and Line 1 platform, though I took the long way around. I didn't get any pictures at Cedarvale, but you can just look at the Eglinton pictures again and it's basically the same thing.

Line 5 concludes at Mount Dennis with connections to a large bus loop, UP Express, and the Kitchener Go Train line. Mount Dennis is the half way point on the UP Express, with express trains to the airport and Union station. It is a bit pricy to take the UP Express, so it's probably better to take the Go train. The Kitchener line also goes all the way to (no not Kitchener) Stratford! Do note that most trains don't go all the way to the end of the line.

Mount Denis is roughly 'V' shaped, with the Line 5 connection at the bottom of the V. This means that the walk from the Go Train to the bus loop is pretty long, but other transfers are quite reasonable. Overall the station is decent, with a lot of windows. I did find the small hallways going to individual Go Train platforms quite narrow. I suspect these will get very crowded during rush hour. The heavy rail platforms themselves are very long. Long enough to accommodate a Go train and an UP train end to end. This is because the boarding level is different between the two trains so they can't use the same platforms. I think they went a bit overboard with platforms here. Room for 4 Go trains and 4 UP trains at the same time really seems a bit much, considering that they run at ~15 min frequencies.

The pedestrian connections could use some work, but it looks like they will get a lot better once construction is done.

Overall I like Line 5 a lot more than Line 6, mostly because it is not painfully slow. The sad part is I think people will learn the wrong lesion, namely that 'the only LRT worth building is underground'. The real lesion should be that 'the only LRT worth building is fast and reliable LRT'. Putting everything underground makes for a fast LRT, but running 10s of km of tunnel in suburbs is not the most cost effective way to build mass transit. With TSP (transit signal priority), sane station spacing, dedicated lanes, and more normal speed limits surface running LRTs can be just as fast and reliable as underground ones.

Stations seem a bit overbuilt and huge imo. There's not way it was cheap to build such huge and deep stations in the middle of a dense city. Despite being huge very little room is put aside for shops, generally only one or two places per station. I'm also really not a fan of the shiny white 'futuristic spaceship' aesthetic. Stations are also all very similar, unlike the older subway stations which all have unique colour palettes. On the plus side signage is pretty good and hallways are wide and open feeling, not nearly as claustrophobic as some of the older subway stations.

What really makes me sad is that Line 5 and 6 aren't just bad in isolation. LRT projects that perform poorly due to obviously stupid design faults give LRTs and transit projects in general a bad rap, which reduces public support and funding for transit in the future. Fortunately not all is lost, like I said in my Line 6 Blag post many of the problems with Line 5 and 6 (and the Toronto streetcars) can be fixed for relatively little and there seems to be some movement in the right direction.
