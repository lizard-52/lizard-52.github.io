The worst named race ever

August 17, 2026:

I went to The Inaugural Ontario Honda Dealers Indy Markham on Saturday and took some pictures.

The event was 3 days (Friday through Sunday) with each subsequent day having more important races. You can see the original schedule here. Friday was intended to be for practice and qualifying, but the track was not done in time and so there was exactly 0 racing done. Fortunately Friday admission was free so nobody lost out too badly, though a lot of people were understandably frustrated. I paid for the cheapest admission on Saturday ($50) and showed up around noon.

I have split this blag post into two sections. The first is all the good, or at least ok, or failing that at least interesting in some way, pictures I took of the races I saw along with some brief commentary. The second part contains my thoughts on the event more broadly.

The Cars:

The first event I saw was the USF2000 Championship, but 'saw' is a bit of a strong word. I spent most of the time looking for somewhere along the track with a decent view so I could take some pictures without 2-4 fences in the way. I did get some decent shots of the Indycar Qualifying laps.


I did find a few places where I could hold my camera above the first fence and look through a gap in the main fence created for emergency vehicle access, but the views were quite limited

Eventually I discovered the objectively correct spot to stand between turns 4 and 5. Enterprise Boulevard goes under the tracks, and there is a decently steep embankment on the south side that let you get a good view down onto the cars. Map for reference.

Thoughts on the Event:

As the name suggests this is the first time there has been an Indy in Markham, having stolen the event from Exhibition Place in downtown Toronto. In general this is the first actually interesting event held in Markham in the few years I have lived in the area. Markham is mostly just endless suburbia, so gets a well deserved reputation for being boring. Currently the plan is to also do the next 4 races in Markham, so that's neat. Overall I think it's a good thing for Markham that this event was done.

You may expect that being the first year of an event would mean that there would be some problems and you would be correct. In no particular order the problems I saw were poor transit connections, bad pedestrian infrastructure, little thought paid to accessibility, badly distributed seating, and questionable event layout. After talking about each of these in more detail I'll also put all my other miscellaneous observations and pictures.

Poor Transit Connections

The elevator pitch for transit probably goes something like "increased Go Train frequency to the station right in the middle of the course along side dedicated shuttle busses" which sounds good on paper. Unfortunately 'increased Go Train frequency' means a train in each direction every 15-30 minutes with some of the extra trips not appearing on Google Maps. I will say that the Go Train station was integrated quite well into the layout of the event so I can't complain too much. There were also combo Go Train + admission tickets which seem like a great deal for the more touristy people. For only $5 more you get (presumably unlimited) access to the Go Train for one or both days of the event. For local residents who have a Presto Card, only go one of the two days, and need to transfer to at least one other mode of transit it's probably not worth it or close to a wash.


Viva Purple is shown in purple, Viva Purple A in pink, YRT route 8 in blue, and the track in red. The Go Train is visible in brown. Dashed lines represent detours. Thank you to Transit Toronto for archiving the relevant route info. For some reason YRT has wiped their website of nearly all info relating to the race.


The story is much worse when it comes to the bus connections. Viva Purple A usually runs right through downtown Markham, but during the event it was canceled so people had to use Viva Purple which runs along Highway 7. This is about a 1km walk away from the event. I really think they should have run Purple A along Enterprise until Birchmount like they have in the few days before and after the event. That would really reduce the time to walk to the bus from the west side. There is also YRT route 8, but the timing did not work out for me and it ended up being faster to just walk to/from Highway 7. I really wish there was a temporary bus loop at the west side of the event. Surely it's not that expensive to set aside some of the gravel parking lot with some temporary barriers and seating. The increased frequency of Viva Purple (a bus every ~5min instead of the usual weekend ~30min) was very nice, it's just a shame the walk was a bit longer than necessary.

There were pretty frequent shuttle busses to the two further parking lots at the IBM offices and Markham Civic Center. I don't have an opinion on these since I didn't use them. They seemed well used, and maybe a bit overcrowded if anything.

Bad Pedestrian Infrastructure

This is a minor nitpick. There was only one place where the pedestrian infrastructure was seriously lacking, that being in the parking lot near the west entrance. Once along Enterprise Boulevard there was a (small) sidewalk to use.

Suboptimal Accessibility

Good luck pushing a stroller or mobility aid through this. The gravel was very coarse and loose.


Getting across the track without being able to climb these stairs was possible, but only during very limited times.


Also these pedestrian overpasses were in pretty bad shape with uneven stairs.

Badly Distributed Seating

Since the tickets with dedicated seats were so expensive most people just bought general admission tickets. All the proper seating in bleachers was not accessible with a general admission ticket so there were a lot of people sitting on the ground right next to ~50% full bleachers.


People, including me, getting creative to see over the fence and crowd. This area was later closed.


People getting creative to see over the fence (again).


There was also a lack of seating indoors.


The best general admission seating. Note the taped off area that was open earlier.


This is an easy problem to solve, just reduce the price of the more expensive tickets or set aside 1-2 of the less desirable bleachers for general admission.

Questionable Event Layout

There were a lot of fences everywhere, some of which I could not discern the purpose of.


Some areas were possible to walk to but apparently out of bounds. Funny enough I saw people sitting under the bridge like 20min after this picture.

Last thing I want to mention is that the bag search/security was very lax and I could have totally snuck something in through security. Also it was trivial to walk into the event without a ticket, the event perimeter was very porous on the east side.
