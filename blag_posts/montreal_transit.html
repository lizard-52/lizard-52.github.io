Montreal Transit Thoughts

Oct 28, 2026:

While in Montreal we took public transit basically everywhere, even though one of my friends had brought his car. This sort of thing was only possible because Montreal has arguable the best transit in Canada. Metro coverage is very good and the bus network is nothing to sneeze at. I am a bit sad that the streetcars were all torn out, but I guess nowhere is perfect.

I have organized this blag into 3 sections. The first is an overview of the types of rapid transit in Montreal, next are the pictures I took, and lastly some assorted thoughts on city design and Montreal specifically.

Transit in Montreal

You can buy day, weekend, or week long transit passes so it's really coinvent as a tourist. You can use an app, but I stuck with the card. I bought my ticket at the metro station right next to our Air B&B. Most (all?) stations have one so that's nice.

A full map of the system as of October 2026 with all the bus routs can be found here.

There are three kinds of trains in Montreal: the Metro, Exo commuter rail, and the REM. The Metro is your standard underground, frequent, metro system. Stations are close by, ~450-600m apart in downtown and ~600m-1km further out. Trains run every 10min or more better usually. Exo is really only usable for people commuting to downtown from the suburbs in the morning and back home in the evening. 3 of the 5 lines run 0 trains on the weekend. Stations are far apart, but unlike the GO system in the Toronto area generally have pretty modest parking lots if they have one at all. REM is the new hotness. It has been opening in phases starting in 2023. The last phase part of the original plan is the connection to the airport which should open in Q4 2027. REM is sort of between commuter rail and a metro, though closer to the metro side of things. Stations are spaced further apart and frequency is a bit worse, but REM is still very usable for a lot of trips in the inner city.

My friends wanted to do things other than ride public transit so I only managed to ride parts of line 1, 2, 4, A3, and A1. WE only ended up taking one bus, but from what I can tell the bus situation seems pretty good overall. After experiencing Vancouver transit I find the lack of trolley buses saddening. There's also a large bike share program, but we didn't use it.

Assorted thoughts

The Montreal Metro and REM together mean that it's very doable to be a tourist in Montreal without renting a car. If you stay at a place within a ~5min walk of a Metro station you can get pretty much anywhere in the inner city in under an hour. Parking downtown is a path to endless suffering so using transit is really the only reasonable thing to do for most trips.

Lines 1 and 2 ranged between busy and packed like a tin of sardines. On the one hand it is nice to see good ridership, but Line 1 could totally do with some capacity improvements. The trains can't get longer or wider, so either they need to remove more seats or increase frequency.

The Metro stations I saw were all from the 60s and 70s and boy can you tell. Despite being quite old, everything was in decent shape and kept nice and clean. Most stations are close to the surface which is good both for reducing costs and convenience for passengers. Smaller and less used stations can be quite small. It's nice to see transit that is sized correctly (looking at you TTC Line 5). Metro stations also tend to have more and less hostile seating compared to the REM stations. Wayfinding in stations is generally good, with the exception of the Gare Centrale to Bonaventure transfer. A neat thing that metro platforms have is a screen that shows the time until the next train as well as which parts of the trains are less busy to encourage people to spread out.

The REM is nice overall, but I have a few complaints.

  • Connections with the Metro range between almost ok and terrible.
  • I don't like the reduction in seating at platforms compared to the metro.
  • Some stations are very far apart. Fortunately there are two infill stations under construction or planning.
  • I found a lot of the stations design repetitive.
  • There's no foamer seat like on the Sky Train!
    •

    Overall I am a big fan of transit in Montreal. I have heard bad things about the BRT and some of the bus routes, but everything we experience was great. Well if you ignore the 711 ride we took across Mount Royal. We were stuck in transit for what felt like 3 hours but was probably more like 20 minutes. Why cars are allowed on Mount Royal at all is a mystery to me.
