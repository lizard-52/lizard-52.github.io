GRT's ION: LRT Done Well

September 6, 2026:

I haven't seen a lot of coverage about the "new" (opened in 2019) LRT line in Kitchener so I figured I would pop over for a look. I'll split this blag post into three sections; background on ION, my experience riding the line, what makes this LRT different from the two new ones (Lines 5 and 6) in Toronto, and finally some closing thoughts.

Background

ION is technically the brand name for GRT's express network, but most people probably just think of the LRT part of the network. Unless otherwise noted I'll refer to the LRT as 'ION'. Construction started in 2014 and was done by 2019. The route is "19km" long with 19 stations, 6 of which are unidirectional (i.e. only serve northbound or southbound trains). The fleet consists of 14 Bombardier Flexity Freedom vehicles, also seen on Line 5 as well as the Edmonton Valley line (among other places). GRT is using them in single car trains so they end up looking a lot like the TTC streetcars. There is provisioning for 2 car trains in the future if needed.

Car Interior

Train Pulling into Waterloo Station.

The route is interesting. Some segments run down the center of a road, some run alongside a road, some parts have dedicated right of ways, and strangest of all some parts share track with CN freight trains. There are three parts of the route where the northbound and southbound routes travel on different roads which is a very interesting design choice. A southbound trip is 15.8km and a northbound trip is 16.2km. Service is every 10 minutes on weekdays and 15 minutes on weekends.

Center running stretch between Northfield and Conestoga

Side running section near City Hall Station.

Dedicated right of way near Waterloo. You can see the gauntlet tracks at stations along the shared portion of track. The two sets of tracks allow wider freight trains to avoid colliding with the platforms.

ION is not done! ION stage 2 will replace the existing bus from the south end of the current LRT to downtown Cambridge with 17km of new LRT tracks. Stage 2 is still in the early stages and hasn't been funded. I look forward to writing another blag post about ION in 5-10 years when it is done.

Riding ION end to end (twice)

I arrived in Kitchener on the GO Train. My initial impressions were not great, the walk to the nearest ION station (Central) is ~600m along a fairly narrow and uneven sidewalk next to a busy stroad. Alternatively you can take the bus like 2 stops, though it comes every half hour. I saw a few people dragging suitcases which didn't look very fun.

Some presumably sad people.

Payment can be done by purchasing tickets or a Easy Go card with a stored value. The card is $4 but lets you buy tickets for $3 instead of the cash fare of $4, so it pays for itself after only 4 trips. You can buy the card from vending machines on station platforms. The fare machines are on the platforms, so you have to tap before you get on or else risk a fine. There are only two tap points on most stations and it's pretty easy to miss them if you are in a hurry. Also the screens on the tap points are very dim and basically impossible to read in even indirect sunlight. Many of them were also damaged.

Can you read this screen? I couldn't.

A lot had these odd circular black spots in the middle.

My first trip was northbound from City Hall Station to Waterloo Public Square Station. I got out to check out 'uptown' which is a very nice recent development spanning a few blocks. Storefronts are directly on the street with nice wide sidewalks. There are some offensively large surface parking lots around the area which is a bit sad, but at least it's not a strip mall. This station is also where the shared portion of track begins. CN trains only use the track in the late evening and early morning so they usually don't conflict with the ION trains.

The station is right next to a mall and plaza. Not bad!

It's a nice, if fairly small area. It is surrounded by large surface parking lots which makes me a bit sad.

After inspecting the area I took the ION to Research and Development station which was an uneventful trip. There's not a whole lot around R&D station, but it looks like they are filling out the industrial park and there is a decent sidewalk/pathway network for an industrial area. Once I was done wandering around Waterloo (they have some neat museums there) I got on at Waterloo station and went to the north end of the line at Conestoga Mall. Conestoga and Waterloo both have large bus loops.

It is mostly parking lots around here, but at least there are sidewalks and trees.

There is GO Bus service here. In typical GO fashion it doesn't use the normal bus stations and is just a small sign strapped to a preexisting pole.

There are a lot of tactile surfaces which is nice, but the bus drivers don't seem to pull up to the indicated part of the kerb.

My penultimate trip on ION was the entire length of the line in the southbound direction. I got lucky with a driver who was a big fan of the sand button and drove like we were running from the cops. That didn't work and the cops (transit security) came aboard to check everyone's fare anyways. See below for a table of times and average speeds between stations.

Things I like:

  • The seats and trains in general are in good condition and reasonably clean.
  • Trains were very good at adhering to the schedule. The latest I saw a train was 1 minute.
  • Stations are small (much smaller than VIVA stations which is pretty funny) but have everything you would want.
  • Over the course of all my trips we waited at 7 red lights, usually for less than 15 seconds. For the southbound trip doing the whole route we waited for 3, so about one every 15 minutes.
  • They used a variant of the classic TTC chime.
  • Trains have signal lights like a bus so they can indicate which way they are going to drivers who are to stupid to notice unable to see the tracks.

I do have a few complaints which are mostly easy to fix.

  • Just like Lines 5/6 there is a super loud beep when the doors want to close. Not sure what's up with that.
  • While stopping at lights is rare, slowing significantly on the approach to one is not. I feel like this could be fixed with adjusting the timing and amount of preemption done. Ideally the trains wouldn't even slow at intersections.
  • The speed limit when making a turn across traffic is 10km/h which seems really low. I'm sure 25km/h would be fine. Click here for a video of a train going around a corner.
  • One of the trains I was on didn't have working station announcements.
  • There's no downtown transit hub where you can connect onto the bus network. One is under construction so this should be fixed by 2029.

Difference with Toronto LRTs

Unlike the TTC streetcar all of ION has dedicated lanes, and unlike both the TTC streetcar and Lines 5/6 ION gets actually working TSP. Higher speed limits (up to 70km/h) combined with drivers not terrified of accelerating faster than a Fiat 126 also really helps. Stations are more widely spaced, with on average a station every ~1000m compared to every ~570m on Line 6. This actually lets the trains hit their top speed and stay there for a minute or two between stations. On average the speed of my southbound trip was 21.6km/h, compared to the glacial 13.7km/h I measured on Line 6.

Another difference is that the GRT trains don't have the useless stop request buttons found on Lines 5/6. They also get free on board Wi-Fi which is neat.

Closing Thoughts

ION shows that it is possible to build a surface running LRT in Canada that isn't terrible. I hope phase 2 gets funding in the near future and goes ahead. It would be really nice to have a rail connection to Cambridge. Maybe Cambridge can also get a GO Train? That would be cool.

The line was surprisingly busy for mid afternoon on a Friday, with most seats full on most of my trips. Ridership figures seem ok, but 2025 totals are ~17% below the peak in 2023 which is odd. For context the 2025 ridership of 3.58 million puts ION in the same ballpark as Pittsburgh Light Rail or Buffalo Metro Rail.
